Amateur Radio for People with Disabilities

Amateur radio is open to everyone, regardless of physical ability, hearing or vision. It provides a means to communicate with others, develop technical skills, and participate in a worldwide network of operators.

PARC supports HandiHam, a long-established organization that helps people with disabilities take part in and succeed in amateur radio. This includes people who are blind, deaf or hard of hearing, or physically impaired, and people with other conditions that make learning or operating a radio more difficult.

HandiHam.org

Getting Started

Amateur radio is more accessible than ever. Voice modes, digital modes and Morse code offer options for every operator. Many tools and radios are compatible with, or can be adapted to, assistive technologies such as screen readers, speech-to-text software and adaptive keyboards.

Full use of the hands, hearing or eyesight is not required to operate a radio. Radios can be operated with foot pedals, speech recognition, braille displays and sip-and-puff devices, and operators who do not speak can communicate using keyboards and text-based modes.

Studying for the Exam

PARC can refer candidates with disabilities to study resources, including:

  • Audio and braille study guides
  • Online courses compatible with screen readers
  • Video content with captions and sign language
  • One-to-one support from mentors and volunteers

Taking the Exam

Candidates who require assistance or accommodations must submit a request before booking. PARC works with the Volunteer Examiner team to provide the support required.

Verbal Examinations

  • Online: an examiner may read each question aloud, up to two times. This must be requested in advance, with the reason for the request. Additional time is allowed when the questions are read to the candidate.
  • In person: an examiner may read the entire examination aloud. Up to one hour is allowed.

Assistance from Others

Another person may assist with setup before the examination begins, but must leave before it starts. Only examiners may be present during the examination. The sole exception is a parent, who may observe from a distance when the candidate is under age 13.

Calculators

  • Online: only the calculator built into ExamTools is permitted. A candidate who cannot use it should consider an in-person examination.
  • In person: candidates may bring a basic calculator with no memory or programmable functions. Calculations are done on the back of the answer sheet.

Examination Results

Candidates are told the number of questions missed. The specific questions missed are not disclosed.

Online Examination Requirements

  • An unexpired physical photo ID, as listed under Acceptable Identification.
  • An FCC Registration Number (FRN), obtained before booking.
  • A quiet, private room, not a vehicle or an outdoor location, with a clear desk and no distracting items in view.
  • Sharing the full screen and scanning the room over Zoom. Candidates who do not consent to this should choose an in-person examination.
